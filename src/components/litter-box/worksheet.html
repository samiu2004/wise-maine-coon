This is a comparison with a general guideline, not a guarantee of fit or acceptance. It is designed for rectangular trays. Curved walls, covered boxes and automatic litter boxes need additional checks of usable space and access.

What the numbers cannot tell you

  • Turning room: check whether your cat can turn and dig without being cramped. There is no width pass/fail score in this worksheet.
  • Entry and cover: consider the step-in height, doorway and headroom. A long box can still be difficult to enter.
  • Changing needs: growing kittens need remeasuring. Older cats and cats with mobility difficulties may need easier access.

A change in toileting behaviour may have a medical cause. Contact your veterinarian rather than relying on a size result to explain it.

How this worksheet works

The length reference is your cat’s nose-to-tail-base measurement multiplied by 1.5. The FelineVMA and iCatCare owner handout gives this sizing guidance. We compare it with the usable inside length so a wide rim does not count as space your cat can use. That interior-measurement approach is our implementation of the guideline.

For width we show your measurement, without inventing a numerical threshold. Units convert using exactly 2.54 centimetres per inch. Display values are rounded to two decimals; the comparison uses the unrounded values.
